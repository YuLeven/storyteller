# Product benchmark: coherent play, not feature count

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-02
**Method:** Review of public first-party product pages, Apple Human Interface Guidelines, and the owner's shared campaign for pace only. No competitor account or feature was hands-on tested. Storyteller evidence below is limited to the separate fictional QA campaign and named automated tests. One owner-authorized live action and its same-turn retry failed proposal validation; the QA campaign had no modeled travel connections for the attempted NPC move, so it had no canonical route. A later live Ask about current weather completed and persisted after reload without changing canonical state. The local Vineyard comparison campaign received no action, GM request, or state change; the user's preexisting browser view reconnected read-only when the local server restarted. No source transcript or campaign state is retained here.

## Product question

Can the player stay immersed in the current scene while the application, rather than a long transcript alone, protects durable world continuity?

The owner’s market-research hypothesis is that an LLM can remember an NPC’s personality while losing practical canon such as travel time and current location. That is a product hypothesis, not a claim about every AI GM. A proposed acceptance exercise is to establish a forty-minute route between two places, leave an employee at the origin, then visit the destination in a later session. The employee must not appear there until a valid move or contact is established. This is a scenario specification, not a test against Vineyard campaign data.

## Interaction guidance applied

Apple’s HIG recommends giving essential information room and placing it by importance; hiding secondary detail until relevant; and keeping routine status feedback near the thing it describes. These principles map well to a tabletop board: orient the player first, disclose deeper details in context, and show a state change where it belongs rather than interrupting narration with bookkeeping. Apple guidance is a design reference, not a claim that Storyteller is an Apple-platform product. [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout) · [Disclosure controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/disclosure-controls) · [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback)

## Campaign pace reviewed against the owner's reference

The user-supplied ChatGPT campaign demonstrates several rhythms in one continuing story: an explicit “days pass” request can resolve routine work across whole days, with progress and relevant interruptions selected for narration; a close inspection or consequential choice can stay at the scale of minutes; and a group scene can include concrete sensory narration and relevant reactions before handing the moment to the player. In the user's comparison, Storyteller returned one short GM beat and one NPC question, leaving out the tasting facts and much of the present group's exchange. The gap is an incomplete scene beat, not simply a short word count. The product goal is responsiveness to intent and stakes, not uniformly short or long GM turns. Players should not have to prompt each incidental action; the GM should stop when a genuine player response or choice is due. The shared transcript and its setting details are intentionally not copied into this repository.

This leads to a product constraint: player agency does not mean the world waits after every single NPC action. The existing Act, Ask, and Pass time controls let the player state an action, ask directly, or authorize time passage; the GM must still resolve the requested scope naturally while leaving the player's actions and decisions to them. Storyteller's action prompt now explicitly lets a grounded shared exchange finish before the handoff, and sensory guidance tells the GM to establish external facts before asking for the player's response. Fake-provider regression verifies those instructions reach action turns; it does not prove generated prose follows them. A new pace toggle is not justified until paired live-play review shows that prompt/context adaptation is insufficient.

## Implications for the next player-facing iteration

1. **Make the current scene legible at a glance.** Give the location, in-world time and weather, immediate situation, and characters actually present clear priority. Show only inventory and campaign resources relevant to the character or genre; put settings and deep campaign records behind nearby, plainly labeled disclosures.
2. **Keep fiction and bookkeeping in their proper places.** Narration and character speech belong in the conversation. Accepted date, weather, location, presence, inventory, and resource changes should update their own board panels with restrained feedback tied to the changed item. Keep the player’s submitted action visible while the GM responds.
3. **Treat relevance and secrecy as one context boundary.** Keep full canon durable server-side, retrieve a compact subset based on current place, present characters, and the player’s action, and keep GM-only facts out of every player-facing projection. Test for both required facts being included and plausible but irrelevant/off-scene details being excluded; do not use request size as proof of context completeness or continuity quality.

## Storyteller evidence and limits

- **Movement, continuity, and pace check (2026-10-08):** In the separate `Quiet Observatory QA — The Last Bell` campaign, the player crossed between an open lower dome and observing platform, then asked the keeper about fresh bell marks. Accepted narration and canonical location/presence panels stayed aligned, and the keeper's reply was concise and uncertain about the cause. However, two explicitly committed moves needed hidden same-turn proposal correction because the first provider result omitted a canonical player-movement operation; one return completed in the first response after the GM movement guidance was clarified. Provider stream stages were about 10.0+10.6 seconds, 7.6+9.5 seconds, and 5.3 seconds (about 1.5 seconds to first output on the last turn). These are three focused observations, not user-visible latency distributions or a pass rate. They show that a reliable correction path protects continuity, while avoidable correction calls still interrupt pace. The next benchmark check is whether the app can reconcile an unambiguous, resolved player move safely without relaxing real barrier, roll, duty, route, visibility, or agency checks. No Vineyard data was used.

- **Game clock and movement follow-up (2026-10-08):** The isolated QA campaign had `04:44, minutes before dawn` as its anchored game time; after six minutes of canon elapsed, the panel still showed 04:44 until numeric-prefix clock advancement was fixed. After refresh it showed 04:50, and a focused live Luna-low six-minute watch turn completed in one stream (about 6.4 seconds, first output about 2.1), with no new chime, one concise keeper reaction, and a current panel time of 04:56. A subsequent step out to the platform also completed in one stream (about 5.6 seconds). Returning through the open doorway while asking the keeper a question still triggered a `location_presence` correction: the final scene and board agreed, but the two provider streams took about 6.6 and 6.9 seconds. Deterministic tests now reconcile a clearly narrated move while preserving other valid changes, with explicit tests for unresolved rolls, hypothetical movement, a real obstacle, and a barrier mentioned behind the player. The accepted correction category does not reveal the rejected draft's precise presence mismatch, so first-response movement reliability remains open. These individual tests do not measure a broad pace, latency, or story-quality distribution. No Vineyard data was used.

- **Recorded QA play:** In one previously observed turn in the separate fictional Quiet Observatory QA campaign, the player asked about job terms at a tavern bar. The GM introduced an employer in narration and dialogue; the scene roster then showed her at that location with the existing companion. The saved player action remained visible while the GM responded. This is one turn, not proof of multi-session continuity, overall prose quality, or comparative performance.
- **Recent live QA recovery check:** An action inviting an NPC from the public corridor into the guest room and its same-turn retry were both rejected during proposal validation; no narration or world-state changes were committed. Read-only inspection confirmed the fictional QA campaign had no modeled travel connections, so the attempted move had no canonical route and rejection was correct. At the time, the UI exposed only generic `invalid_response` guidance; it now also offers a localized route hint for proposal-validation failures without exposing private validator details. A later, simple Ask about the current weather completed through the configured ChatGPT-plan connection in about 12 seconds. Its story entry persisted after reload, while date, time, weather, and location remained unchanged. This confirms the basic no-state-change Ask path and reload persistence, but it does not verify a successful stateful GM proposal or story-quality parity. The failed action was superseded by the successful Ask. Do not treat either sample as a latency benchmark. The imported comparison campaign and Vineyard source were not accessed for this check.
- **Tracked-progress QA (2026-10-07):** On the separate Quiet Observatory campaign, a multi-day catalog montage claimed the tracked count rose from zero while the panel stayed at zero. The app had not yet enforced a link between that qualitative claim and an explicit panel operation. A later player request explicitly asked for the number to be recorded; the new guard rejected an omitted change before it could commit, and a same-turn retry plus focused correction produced a +6 delta. The final narration said six plates, the board and event receipt showed six, and the next reload retained both; game time advanced exactly two days and the clock mystery remained unresolved. The first automatic correction attempt hit a separate `unsupported_capability` provider error, so the player still needed to use the UI retry. This one QA sequence validates the guarded explicit-update path, not general prose/state agreement, first-try reliability, or a comparative story-quality result. No Vineyard data was accessed.
- **Live stateful and cross-session QA (2026-10-02):** In the separate Quiet Observatory campaign, “advance ten minutes” first failed proposal validation without applying narration or state changes; retrying the same saved turn succeeded. The canonical elapsed-time ledger and board advanced exactly ten minutes, while Rowan's place and the public weather fields stayed unchanged. The turn survived reload. Starting a new session retained the same clock, location, and earlier story in its board and GM context. A cross-session Ask for Mira's location first failed validation; retry then correctly answered that she was in the Observatory Guest Corridor waiting to go to the kitchen for sherry and oatcakes before comparing charts. That answer and the unchanged clock persisted after reload. These two turns demonstrate the stateful/retry/continuity path on the live local route, but both required a retry; the small sample does not establish first-try reliability, latency, scene pacing, or parity with the in-chat reference. No Vineyard source or imported comparison campaign was used.
- **Follow-up QA correction (2026-10-02):** During the subsequent reload/layout check, a failed “A day passes” turn appeared in the later Quiet Observatory session without an intentional submission. It committed no canon and was not retried; the cause has not been established. Reviewing the earlier session then showed that the later failure and pending input leaked across the session boundary. The LiveView now filters the displayed current turn by session, with a regression test. Treat the functional MVP gate as open until the unexpected submission/recovery behavior and remaining basic play checks are resolved; do not count this as a model-quality or benchmark result.
- **Behavior tests:** A fake-provider sparse-scene observation test checks that a brief ambient impression leaves world state, inventory, character presence, elapsed time, revision, and prior events unchanged; the serialized request stays under its 10,000-byte test ceiling. A production-boundary regression covers direct and indirect scene questions over a 2,400-event/100-session fixture in English, Spanish, and French: current-place detail is retained while connected-place decoys are excluded, within a 24,000-byte preflight ceiling. Other synthetic fixtures cover 240-event retrieval and selected cross-session schedule, promise, decision, work-plan, and next-step cues with decoys. These are bounded cue/retrieval tests, not general semantic search or proof of live-GM quality.
- **Usage accounting and human review:** Serialized bytes are a request diagnostic, not a token count or outcome to minimize. A human still needs to review live writing quality and repeat representative tasks across resource, exploration, and social campaigns. For pace, use matched routine-interval, focused-inspection, active-dialogue, and ensemble-scene tasks; record how many player prompts it takes to reach the next genuine decision, whether the requested time/scope resolves, whether the GM supplies sensory facts, and any agency/continuity errors. Compare the shared ChatGPT reference qualitatively, not as a live controlled benchmark.
- **Scope boundary:** No competitor was tried hands-on, and no private campaign text is included here. The visual browser experience and multi-session scenario above are not a controlled comparative study.

## Next benchmark tasks

Use the same player tasks in future QA: identify the current place and who is present; inspect owned items/resources; retrieve an old commitment; distinguish what the player knows from GM-only context; and recover after a failed turn. Record task success, time-to-understanding, continuity errors, and request size separately. Do not collapse these into a feature count or market claim.

Product decision: keep the immediate board limited to the current scene, present characters, and the campaign's relevant inventory/resources. Keep player control explicit while letting GM pacing adapt to the scene's scope. Prioritize a matched travel/presence continuity task and an old-commitment recall task over additional maps, dashboards, or provider-side memory features. This is an inference from the published patterns and Storyteller's goal; no competitor was used hands-on, and no product was ranked.
