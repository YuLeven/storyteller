# Product benchmark: coherent play with a light context budget

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-01  
**Evidence boundary:** This is a first-party public-documentation review, not a hands-on competitor trial. Storyteller was inspected in the separate fictional QA campaign 35/session 36. Campaign 34 (Vineyard) was not opened, and this review made no live GM request.

## What comparable products teach us

| Area | AI-native reference: Friends & Fables | System-agnostic reference: LegendKeeper | Storyteller decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Durable memory and retrieval | Friends & Fables describes atomic memories, retrieval of older relevant memories, and entity mentions that force an entity into the next context. Its 2024 engine notes say too many memories can become expensive or confusing; its 2025 memory update shifts to fewer event memories plus embedding-assisted search over older ones. | LegendKeeper emphasizes linked lore, full-text search, reusable pages and timelines rather than an AI-maintained conversation memory. | Keep the canonical database authoritative. The current compiler filters player-managed public notes by meaningful word overlap with the action, present characters and place, while retaining their stable metadata and all GM-authored/private canon. This deterministic lexical gate avoids resending unrelated notes without another model call, but synonyms and implicit references need scenario tests. Preserve rare, durable facts when a prior model proposal omits them, while keeping the player's manual memory tool small. The remaining gap is lower-effort discovery of old details without requiring the player to become an archivist. |
| Player-facing board and navigation | Friends & Fables describes putting location, nearby NPCs, quests and easier action/roll access in its standalone play surface ([product update](https://fables.gg/blog/a-new-chapter)). | LegendKeeper organizes lore around connected wiki pages, maps, chronicle/timeline views and calendars. It supports hiding material from players ([features](https://www.legendkeeper.com/features/)). | Keep the live board focused on current place, surroundings, present characters, commitments, inventory and genre-specific resources. Put deeper canon, settings and corrections behind relevant disclosures. Do not build a general-purpose worldbuilding wiki into the MVP. |
| State and flexibility | Friends & Fables describes itself as a D&D-inspired platform, highlighting 5e tactical play, character statistics and game-state tracking ([about](https://fables.gg/about)). | LegendKeeper describes a system-agnostic worldbuilding tool and flexible calendar/timeline system, but its public feature list is an organizer rather than a validated AI game engine. | Combine flexible campaign-defined panels with typed records for facts that must be enforced (place, travel, presence, item ownership, quantities and resources). A genre-agnostic wine/cash ledger should work beside a dungeon inventory without imposing 5e rules. Narration alone never changes tracked canon. |
| Hidden context | Both products describe keeping selected lore or secrets hidden from players. | LegendKeeper documents granular permissions and player-hidden map/wiki details. | Keep hidden GM context in separate server-side projections and test every player-facing surface and backup. Public notes and private GM knowledge must never share a presentation path that can leak a secret. |

Friends & Fables describes a campaign engine separate from the GM persona and notes that its ACE-1 design uses atomic memories and relevant retrieval; its later memory notes describe retrieving older relevant campaign memories. This validates the direction of an application-owned memory/retrieval layer, but it is a vendor description, not independent evidence of campaign quality. ([ACE-1 overview](https://fables.gg/blog/introducing-ace-1-the-engine-powering-the-best-ai-ttrpg-experiences), [memory retrieval notes](https://fables.gg/patch-notes/memories-v2)).

LegendKeeper's public feature list emphasizes linked lore, maps, chronicle and timeline views, search, and permission controls. It is a useful reference for flexible campaign organization and player-safe secrets, but it is not a like-for-like AI GM benchmark. ([features](https://www.legendkeeper.com/features/), [RPG campaign manager](https://www.legendkeeper.com/rpgs/)).

## Design principles applied

Apple's Human Interface Guidelines recommend matching feedback to the importance of the change, keeping routine status passive, using progressive disclosure to hide details until relevant, and making motion brief, purposeful and cancelable. Storyteller applies those ideas to tabletop play: the story stays central; state changes update their own panels; motion signals a meaningful change without turning every ledger update into a chat interruption; niche controls remain available near the records they affect. These are design references, not claims that Storyteller is an Apple-platform product. ([Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback), [Disclosure controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/disclosure-controls), [Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion), [Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles)).

## Storyteller hands-on finding and next actions

The QA session shows a useful fiction-first loop: the player can ask what they notice or explicitly pass time; travel updates the game-time label; the current location and situation remain on the board. In the last observed look-around, however, the GM answered that there were no new details, repeated that the bar was the place to ask, and the same explanation appeared again in the current-situation panel. The timeline and board were individually readable, but the answer did not add a strong tabletop cue for what the player could inspect or do next. That is the next narration-quality target: concise, vantage-grounded answers, no near-verbatim repetition between the conversation and persistent scene panel, and a natural hand-back of agency. It should be fixed in GM response guidance and tested with fake providers; do not spend a live plan request to test prompt wording.

| Priority | Product action | Evidence of success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Now | Make look-around and direct scene questions answer from the character's current vantage, avoid reprinting board facts, and give the player a natural next choice without forcing one. | Behavioral tests prove unchanged canon/time for questions, concise non-duplicative replies, and no artificial discovery. Manual read of the isolated QA flow confirms the story and current-situation panel complement each other. |
| MVP | Improve retrieval for durable details established in older conversation, with explicit protection for typed canon and a context-cost regression. Minimize manual memory maintenance. | Across multiple sessions, relevant old facts appear when asked about while unrelated facts stay out; ordinary calls remain within the conservative byte ceiling; private details never reach public output. |
| MVP | Validate several genres with the same play contract: a vineyard/resource campaign, a location-constrained exploration campaign, and a social or historical scene. | Each scenario can track only the state its genre needs, preserve character presence and commitments, and keep the GM's replies coherent without importing another game's mechanics. |
| Defer | General-purpose maps/wiki authoring, tactical combat grids, party collaboration and broad provider integrations. | Revisit only after single-player narrative play is coherent and the current board proves insufficient in actual play. |

## How to keep this benchmark honest

- Competitor statements above are public vendor documentation reviewed on the date shown; no account-based feature was hands-on tested.
- The Storyteller observation is from the fictional QA campaign only. It is a qualitative issue report, not a comparative score.
- Use the same tasks for future hands-on comparisons: find current location and present characters, inspect inventory/resources, retrieve one old commitment, distinguish public knowledge from GM secrets, and recover after a failed turn.
- Judge coherence, time-to-understanding, player agency and context use. Do not compare feature counts or treat vendor marketing as measured performance.
