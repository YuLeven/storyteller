# UX acceptance brief

This brief defines observable acceptance for the locally hosted, single-player Storyteller MVP. It follows the committed `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`; it does not define new game mechanics. A screen is accepted when the player can understand what is happening, choose what to do, and recover without losing or duplicating campaign state.

## Defining promise: a consistent world, at a deliberate context cost

Storyteller's defining promise is that the campaign stays coherent because the application maintains structured canon, not because the GM model is expected to remember every detail in a long transcript. Persisted characters, places, connections, presence, inventory, and campaign resources are authoritative; narration is accepted only when its proposed changes agree with that canon. The full campaign history remains durable, while each GM request receives only a compact, relevant projection so consistency does not require sending the whole campaign every turn.

- **Travel and presence:** in a fictional vineyard campaign, the Finca and Bodega are distinct places joined by a canonical 40-minute connection. An employee who stays at the Finca cannot speak or act as if physically at the Bodega. A proposed move must follow a persisted or same-turn accepted route; the application computes and records its duration from the connection instead of trusting a duration asserted by the model. A same-turn newly discovered place, route, and arrival is accepted only when the normalized route exists and validates.
- **Active GM-character duty:** an owner can assign or revise a named active duty for an existing or new GM character only when that character has a known current place. A blank duration means indefinite; a finite duration is authored in in-world minutes and stored as an absolute release minute anchored to the persisted elapsed-world clock at assignment/edit. While the persisted clock is below that threshold, movement to another place fails even with a canonical route. Validate against the pre-turn clock, so a departure's own proposed time advance cannot bypass the duty. Any earlier accepted turn that advances elapsed time to or past the threshold completes it; a subsequent route-valid departure then succeeds and still adds the server-computed route duration. Setting an existing duty's remaining time to 0 completes it at the current stored minute; clearing its name releases it. Same-place actions and unassigned/available characters work normally. Duty details, deadlines, and completion are GM-private and absent from the player board, public projections, and story events. Releasing or changing it requires an out-of-character reason and current campaign revision, creates a private authoring audit entry, and changes neither story history nor game time. Stale revisions and campaigns with an unresolved turn reject duty edits. Backup schema v11 preserves finite-duty thresholds and remote-message paths/events, validates duty locations against the backup clock, and continues to import v1–v10; v9 and earlier treat existing duties as indefinite. Recurring schedules and free-form schedule parsing are not part of this guarantee.
- **Elapsed world time:** the durable campaign clock is an integer minute total, separate from free-form fantasy date/time labels. Every accepted move advances it by at least the server-computed route floor. Sequential legs sum per character; simultaneous characters share elapsed time, so use the largest per-character sum. `time_advance_minutes` is the proposed total turn duration, including travel; commit `max(model total, canonical travel floor)` to avoid counting a narrated route twice. It must be a bounded non-negative integer, positive for Time passage and exactly zero for Ask GM. Failed or rejected turns leave the clock untouched. Show minutes elapsed beneath the current time label; when the accepted date/time label changes, re-anchor the cue to that exact new label. Never parse “First watch” or infer a date rollover.
- **Anchor the opening scene:** a fresh campaign's first GM scene establishes a canonical public place for the player and explicitly places any NPCs who speak or act there. Once a character has a known place, every move to a different place needs a graph route. A legacy character with no known origin can receive a first recorded placement; because there is no prior location to measure from, that initial placement has no computed journey duration.
- **Cross-session consistency:** move a character or item in one session, begin/resume another session, and verify that the board and GM context show the same persisted location, ownership, and quantities. An old transcript line cannot restore stale state or override the canonical record. Verify private places, routes, and details remain excluded from public projections and exports/imports preserve the canon.
- **Durable story memory:** preserve a concise public fact that is not yet a typed ledger value (for example, the group's agreed work plan). Add or correct it outside the fictional timeline, with an auditable reason. Start a later session and use a fake-provider turn that proposes no continuity changes: the memory must remain on the campaign board and in GM context. It must not create a chat message or advance game time. Unrelated memory must not be injected into every request, and player-visible memory must never include hidden GM-only facts.
- **Indirect social commitments:** save a typed future-meeting and future-reply commitment in one session, then ask about it in a later session with indirect English, Spanish, and French wording. The matching commitment detail reaches the fake provider; same-topic facts and unrelated commitments retain only identity/status metadata. The request remains within the configured 24,000-byte preflight bound. Retrieval uses a small reviewed cue vocabulary, not general semantic search.
- **Bounded GM context:** grow a test campaign with substantial unrelated history and graph detail. Retain the recent scene and current canon, retrieve a relevant fact older than the latest 40 events, and verify unrelated history does not make the request grow without bound. A representative ordinary question/action after at least 27 modest events must reach the fake provider under the default 24,000-byte preflight bound. Enforce the configured conservative serialized-byte input bound before calling any provider. An active duty remains in compact GM context through history compaction while unrelated history and profile prose are dropped first; do not resend the full transcript. Observe aggregate input/output token usage from successful provider responses when available; current usage is transient local telemetry, not a saved per-turn report. Exact section-level tokenizer counts are not available through the current OAuth account, so section byte sizes must never be presented as exact token counts. Keep the complete history locally and ensure diagnostics contain only numeric sizes/counts, never prompts, campaign text, identifiers, or hidden values.
- **Recovery and observability:** reject impossible movement or off-scene public dialogue before appending story events; preserve the previous canon on rejection. Accepted movement is visible in the relevant state panel/audit detail and future context, not as a system-style chat interruption.

Travel connections, movement/presence validation, and the persisted elapsed-world-time ledger are implemented. Legacy sessions with no canonical player place retain compatibility until their first placement. Fresh opening scenes must leave the player at a known or newly created public place, and any public NPC who speaks or acts must be present in the scene; an invalid opening proposal writes no timeline event. The clock records server-computed route time, persists across sessions, and reaches both GM context and the player board without parsing free-form labels or rolling dates forward. Finite owner-authored duties now store absolute release thresholds against this clock and are checked against pre-turn time; only a separate, earlier accepted time-advancing turn can make a duty available before a later departure. Duty completion is derived from current elapsed time and remains hidden from player-facing views. Backup v11 persists these thresholds and remote-message paths/events, validates active duty location, and imports v1–v10 with no path ledger by default; v1–v9 duties remain indefinite. The deterministic compiler and conservative byte preflight are implemented. Continuity detail retrieval now applies to GM-authored and player-managed records in public and GM-private scopes: each request includes at most eight relevant details per scope, retains stable identity/status metadata for other notes, and marks omissions in context completeness so the GM treats missing details as unknown. Explicit duties survive remote-character compaction; less relevant history/profile prose is removed first, and the full durable transcript is not sent on every turn. Broad/open-ended actions can retrieve up to eight older events anchored to current or connected places and current-scene characters/speakers; concrete queries keep a stricter action-term gate. This is bounded lexical retrieval, not semantic intent detection, and same-scene false matches or phrasing gaps remain possible. A fixed-policy size regression at 27 events was corrected by a 34.6% policy reduction. The 240-event long-campaign fixture retains three labeled relevant events and excludes seven hard decoys. A separate 2,400-event fixture spanning 100 synthetic sessions preserves the older Finca/Bodega route-and-duty fact, all 12 recent turns, and canonical locations, duties, route, and inventory; it stays within the same configured bound and 2 KB of a 12-event baseline. These synthetic cases measure bounded retrieval under specific labels, not general semantic understanding. Curated retrieval covers selected wine/seasonal concepts and employment schedule/promise questions in English, Spanish, and French, with bridge-hour and toll-payment decoys. It remains a bounded lexical system rather than general multilingual or semantic search; arbitrary paraphrase gaps remain. This snapshot does not test normal retry while usage is available; fake-provider LiveView tests cover that transition and confirm resume itself makes no provider call. The campaign board now includes player-managed public story memory as a small backstop for facts that are not yet represented by a typed ledger. Up to eight 300-character notes stay visible across sessions and in relevant GM context even when the provider proposes no continuity updates. The application refuses GM proposals that try to change those notes; the player can edit or remove them through a reasoned out-of-character form. These writes have an audit trail, do not add story messages, and do not advance in-world time. Backup schema v7 preserves notes without fabricating story-event provenance, and v6 backups remain importable. ## Out-of-character canon corrections

- From the active session, the player can open a collapsed correction panel and choose one tracked public target: inventory item details/quantity/owner, a typed public campaign resource, or a public character's location among known public places. Public characters with unknown locations are selectable for repair; characters in GM-private places and non-public location IDs remain hidden.
- Require a concrete reason and show a before/after receipt outside the fictional timeline. The receipt identifies the target and repair values, remains durable across refreshes, and does not create a GM message or pretend the correction happened in-world.
- A correction must not change the in-world clock, rewrite old story events, or expose GM-private inventory, resources, characters, places, or location IDs. Corrected canonical state must be visible to subsequent player panels and GM prompts, including after switching sessions.
- Submit the current play-state revision with each form. Reject stale forms without applying them; lock the state during pending/resolving/awaiting-roll turns and reject corrections then, so a GM proposal built from pre-correction context cannot overwrite the repair. A failed turn can be corrected and retried using the saved action against fresh context.
- **Implemented:** these behaviors are covered by isolated service, backup round-trip, and LiveView tests. The additive correction migration has since been applied to the local development database without rewriting campaign records. Campaign 35/session 36 loads, but its turn 19 is superseded and turns 22–24 are complete; no failed/retryable turn exists, so the retry investigation made no additional provider call. Campaign 34/Vineyard was not accessed.

## Campaign library

- The library lists the player's campaigns and provides clear actions to create, open/resume, and archive one. Empty and loading states explain the next available action.
- Each campaign shows enough identifying information to distinguish it from others and to choose the correct one. Opening or switching campaigns never mixes their characters, timeline, or world state.
- A campaign exposes its sessions as resumable segments of one continuous campaign history. Starting or switching sessions preserves prior turns and canonical state. Campaign details give direct access to the active session and explain when starting another will complete it.
- Campaign edits keep entered character voice and mannerism text through LiveView validation and save errors. The voice section stays open while it contains a draft or saved guidance, making edits visible through the save and reload path. The required correction-reason field appears before the editable setup so it cannot be missed at the end of a long form; if a save is rejected for a missing reason, voice drafts remain visible so the player can finish the save. After a successful save, reopening the editor shows the saved values and future GM context uses them without rewriting earlier turns.
- Backup restore lives in a collapsed **Campaign persistence** section at the bottom of the campaign library, so recovery still works when no campaign exists to open. Downloading a campaign backup lives in the same collapsed section on that campaign's detail page, below its sessions. The library's primary path stays focused on creating, opening, and resuming stories. Both sections explain that backup files contain private GM context.

## Create campaign

- A guided flow collects the campaign title, story premise, setting, tone, narration language, player character details, optional GM-controlled characters, starting location/date/weather, and any campaign-specific mechanics. It makes required versus optional inputs clear without inventing rules or requiring unsupported character fields.
- GM-character setup can optionally record each character's starting place as canonical presence. Use the campaign's exact opening-location name to place someone with the player; a different name creates a separate public place without inventing a route. A blank field leaves the character's origin unknown, and the game must not silently place every character with the player.
- Setup is grouped into four compact steps—story, player character, starting scene, and optional people/details—then a review before creation. Back and forward navigation preserve entered values, and validation returns to the relevant step. Keep one form flow, avoid losing work during the transition, and do not hide configured optional rows.
- Optional player facts, starting inventory, GM characters, and campaign panels are explicitly labeled optional. They begin collapsed when empty, then open when a row is added so the main setup path stays easy to scan without hiding configured content.
- The player can review the assembled setup before creating the campaign. Editing or returning between steps preserves entered content; validation identifies the affected field and explains how to fix it.
- Successful creation opens the new campaign in a ready-to-play state. The chosen narration language is stored separately from the interface locale.
- Exact wizard step order, field-by-field validation, and which setup fields are required remain design choices unless specified by product decisions.

## Play screen and player agency

- The play screen presents a readable, chronological timeline that distinguishes player input, GM narration, speaker-attributed NPC dialogue, public remote messages, relevant GM-controlled character activity, and rolls. A remote message has a visibly distinct style and sender label, and its stored event type is not `npc_dialogue`. New completed turns appear without a full-page reload. The initial recent window is bounded; a clear action loads preceding campaign history in chronological order until the earliest event is reached.
- The play surface foregrounds the character's immediate situation and campaign-relevant facts. Interface preferences such as English/Spanish/French and the connected-account GM model belong in Settings, not beside the story as a primary action. The model choice must be drawn from the connected account's available catalog, persist locally, default to Automatic, and apply to new turn resolutions; controls stay off the play board. In the story, show canonical game date/time at the first event of each turn, then repeat it within that turn only when the clock changes. Keep those markers correct across sessions and when older pages load; never show real-world timestamps. Avoid other repeated labels, setup/admin details, and state already clear from the board; keep a direct path to deeper reference when the player wants it.
- Looking around or inspecting something reports newly noticed or specifically inspected details from the character's current public vantage point. It does not replay the scene, repeat the situation panel, or echo the previous answer. A follow-up observation adds at most one supported new detail; when canon is sparse, the GM may add one brief, non-actionable ambient impression about light, indistinct sound, smell, or general feel when consistent with the known place, time, and weather. Keep ambient details source-free. Never invent a person, item, exit/route, hazard, clue, service, or other actionable fact; those require accepted canon, with accepted presence for a person. If an action depends on an untracked feature, the GM states uncertainty or asks. Ask GM leaves fictional time and canonical state unchanged.
- Small scene cues help ground time and conditions in the imagined world (for example, a moon behind clouds with mist at midnight). Story entries arrive gently in the already-paced sequence, and a changed panel value gets a brief cue in place. Keep words authoritative and readable; imagery and motion support the game instead of demanding attention.
- NPC speech is visually attributed to its speaker. Visible activity communicates what relevant NPCs do; it does not reveal private facts, hidden motives, offstage events, or other GM-only state.
- A compact header shows current player-visible world state, including location and in-world date/time and weather where available. Configured campaign panels show their labelled, typed, unit-aware visible fields; the vineyard may use cash, wine inventory, and vine inventory. Panels must not expose private values.
- Public tracked resources with a saved change can expand a collapsed “Last changed” detail beside the value. It shows the accepted before/after, public reason, and in-world time. Private panel values and private reasons never appear in that detail; no state-change receipt is added to the story timeline.
- Public place and character changes can expand a collapsed “Last changed” detail on their relevant board cards. Show only accepted public state: a place's creation, a character's visible fact update, or a character's move between known public places, with before/after values, an available safe reason, and in-world time. Exclude GM-private places, presence, facts, and reasons; keep these receipts in panels rather than chat.
- Each date, time, and weather fact has one canonical value across the GM's hidden context, the compact header, and the scene board. Legacy aliases and conflicting persisted values must not create duplicate or contradictory facts in play.
- A player can find a concise character board during the scene: current place first, its surroundings, the latest public GM narration as the immediate situation, character details they know, current companions' visible activity, and owned inventory/resources. The latest narration updates as play continues and never includes GM-private context. This should answer “where am I, what is happening, what do I carry or control?” without reading the entire timeline first.
- The campaign premise remains available as an expandable reference during play, without leaving the session or repeating it in the event timeline.
- The Plus OAuth game-master route supports streamed text but not generated scene images. Do not add paid API image calls implicitly; scene illustration remains an open integration for a local image generator or player-provided campaign art.
- Campaigns can model distinct carried items and consumables with stable identity, owner, quantity, unit, and any campaign-relevant safe details. Resource ledgers also support non-item values such as vineyard cash, wine stock, and vines. The same interaction works for different campaign genres without hardcoded vineyard or dungeon rules.
- A text composer lets the player submit an action or speech. Pending submission prevents accidental duplicates and makes the submitted text and resulting turn status understandable.
- Contextual play nudges include looking around, asking a direct question, waiting at the current place, and explicitly letting time pass. Let time pass includes a **Pass a few days** nudge that stops at the next meaningful player decision; the player can still request a custom duration in the composer. Time passage preserves a duration the player stated, including multiple days, and may advance world or NPC activity; it never chooses or narrates the player's actions, speech, thoughts, movement, or rolls. The structured clock contract in the defining-promise acceptance checks governs elapsed minutes; the calendar remains the campaign's free-form label.
- A newly submitted player action stays visibly in the story while the GM works, including after reconnect; when the canonical event arrives, it replaces the pending preview without duplication. New GM, NPC, and world beats reveal in a paced sequence with a clear show-all control. Initial history is not replayed, and loading older history preserves the reader's position.
- The story reads like a tabletop conversation: player actions, natural GM narration, direct character dialogue, and relevant roll prompts. A completed response feels like one coherent GM turn, not four or five abrupt, disjointed messages. When a character has related things to say, combine them into one concise utterance and speech bubble; avoid filler and repeated lines. Do not add system-style chat entries for memory, inventory, location, resources, or character-record mutations. Those changes appear in their corresponding panels and receive a brief, restrained animation; the durable audit record remains available to the application without cluttering the conversation.
- New characters are introduced through the GM's scene narration and their own dialogue. Their structured facts update the character panel without “new character” notices or stat dumps. Date/weather changes are described naturally while the canonical world bar updates and briefly signals the changed value.
- On roomy desktop viewports, the play board fills the window: the story is the only overflowing vertical reading surface while world facts, campaign resources, current scene, inventory, and composer remain in place. Keep the essential world rail visible; clamp its immediate-situation preview while the full narration remains in story history. Narrow or short viewports keep normal document scrolling so controls remain reachable. Loading earlier story preserves the visible event anchor.
- If campaign artwork is supported, reveal each image beside its story beat at the same readable pace and let the player skip ahead. The current Plus OAuth route cannot generate images; a local generator or player-provided campaign art must be selected before paced image cards can be implemented without adding API billing.
- The board exposes active public campaign memory as expandable notes with a concise add/edit/retract path; it is clearly separated from private GM context. Saving a public memory note records its reason and audit trail without a system-style timeline message or in-world time change. Durable private GM context remains hidden from every player-facing projection, and public resolutions/retractions remain traceable without exposing private reasoning.
- The GM responds to player choices with natural consequences, preserves established facts, and returns control to the player. The UI never writes the player's actions, speech, thoughts, or choices on their behalf. It does not present a proposed state change as canonical before the application accepts it.
- Location, character presence, inventory ownership, and resource quantities are canonical persisted state. Narration alone cannot change them: accepted state deltas reference stable records, are validated, committed once with their turn, and appear in a traceable history. If a turn records multiple player movements, the final canonical place and world location agree in the player board and later GM context. Numeric campaign balances use signed deltas applied against the locked current value; text/status/date fields use typed set operations. Each resource history entry shows the field, before and after values, operation, unit, and grounded reason. A read-only account review creates no balance change.
- Place connections and travel durations, plus character location and presence, constrain who can plausibly appear in a scene. A character away from the current place does not enter the conversation for incidental dialogue unless accepted movement or an established communication path explains their presence.
- Establish a public sender-to-player communication path only through an explicit accepted path operation tied to the known NPC's public in-scene dialogue; its cited basis text must exactly match that line, and the line must plainly name the usable endpoint so the player can use it without guessing. `public_changes` and its case/separator-normalized aliases cannot seed or edit the path ledger. Each accepted establishment or deactivation has a public before/after `state_change` audit record in the same transaction, outside the story timeline. A delivery references the previously saved active path ID and matching sender. Missing, inactive, and sender-mismatched paths are rejected atomically. GM context includes at most 12 active paths, with query-matched routes and then recent routes ranked first. A remote delivery alone does not move the NPC or change world time or routes. Ordinary off-scene dialogue/activity presence validation remains unchanged. Path canon round-trips in campaign backup state, and older backups without a path ledger remain importable.
- Long campaigns retain separate public and GM-private continuity summaries. A bounded recent-event window keeps each GM request practical, while older events remain in durable storage and are available to the player through cursor-paginated campaign history. Pages use immutable event sequences, appear exactly once in chronological order, and remain loaded during live updates. Summaries support retrieval but never replace the authoritative item, location, character, and resource records. Private continuity must never appear in player projections.
- Each GM request uses a bounded, relevance-ranked projection of canonical facts and retrieved history rather than the full campaign transcript. Keep exact canonical state in the database and fail safely if essential context cannot fit; never silently ask the model to fill a canon gap. A growing unrelated history must stay within a conservative per-model serialized-byte bound while relevant older facts remain retrievable. Capture provider-reported aggregate input/output usage after completion, and keep diagnostics private-text-free.
- Narrow layouts keep the timeline, composer, die when relevant, world header, and access to campaign panels usable without horizontal page overflow. A compact in-page navigation provides a direct jump to the active turn composer, campaign story, combined current-scene/place card, and inventory, plus objectives and tracked resources when those public sections have content. World date, time, weather, and location appear once in the compact header; the scene card gives the place, surroundings, and people present. The exact panel arrangement remains open. Dedicated keyboard, screen-reader, and formal contrast review is V1 work.

## Player-click D20 flow

- If an uncertain, consequential player action needs a roll, the timeline states what is being tested and the difficulty or target before asking for the player's roll. Ordinary actions do not trigger a roll merely to add activity.
- The player initiates the roll with an explicit D20 click. The interface clearly indicates when a roll is awaited, accepted, and resolved; the recorded result is visible in the turn history. The server-generated result is applied exactly once.
- The GM does not silently roll for a player-controlled character. The current MVP lets the player initiate their own D20 result; add a distinct, auditable GM roll only if a campaign's rules require it.
- The exact dice presentation, animation, and any campaign-specific interpretation of targets remain open choices; the interface must not imply mechanics that the campaign has not defined.

## Progress, failure, and reconnect

- While a GM response is in progress, show a clear pending/progress state and keep the player from submitting a duplicate action. Do not imply that a turn is complete before the response is validated and committed.
- On timeout, invalid response, or other recoverable failure, explain that resolution did not complete, preserve the prior canonical state, and offer a clear retry path. State that retry continues the same saved action; if the player already rolled, say the saved result will be reused. Retrying does not duplicate the action, event, roll, or state change.
- If a turn is waiting for a player roll, a refresh or reconnect restores that same pending turn and roll request. If a turn completed, reconnect restores the completed timeline and current state. The player should not have to infer whether an action was lost or applied twice.
- When account usage prevents a GM request, show one clear account-wide pause with a link to usage settings and an explicit Resume requests action. This is the only visible explanation of the account limit. Keep the failed turn calm and focused on the preserved action or opening scene; show that same action in the story and keep its Retry this turn control in place but disabled until the player resumes requests. Remove secondary pause warnings from the composer and avoid repeating the limit in status announcements. Resuming only clears the pause; it must not call the provider. After resume, enable retry for the same saved turn, preserve any D20 result and canonical state, and wait for the player to retry explicitly. Do not imply a specific reset time, fictionalize the limit as game energy, or silently switch to paid API usage. Other campaigns may still attempt requests against the shared plan allowance.

## Localization (MVP)

- English, Spanish, and French are available across campaign setup and play, including navigation, labels, validation and recovery messages, dates, numbers, dice labels, and campaign-panel chrome. The initial UI locale is English, and the language control in Settings changes the persisted interface locale.
- Changing the UI locale updates interface text and formatting without translating or rewriting stored turns. GM narration follows the campaign's narration language; user-authored names, story text, and custom field labels remain as entered. Untranslated custom content has a clear fallback.

## Accessibility (V1 — deferred)

- All actions, including form navigation, campaign/session selection, turn submission, and the D20 click, are keyboard operable with a visible focus indicator. Focus moves predictably after navigation, validation errors, and turn updates.
- When submitting disables the Send button, verify keyboard focus remains understandable and reachable. Do not move focus when the player is elsewhere; this specific focus check still needs a manual keyboard pass.
- New story entries are exposed through a polite, additions-only live region so assistive technology can announce appended events without rereading the full campaign timeline.
- A single, always-mounted polite status region announces saved/reconnecting turns, active resolution, the requested player roll, a recorded result during after-roll resolution, failure/retry, and completion. Completion is announced only after the current connection observes a turn leave the open state; reopening an already completed session does not present old history as a new completion. Older-history loading does not announce past events as new story; only a small recent portion is in the polite live region.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences by skipping the reveal wait and entry animations while preserving immediate access to the same story and panel state.
- Text and controls remain readable at narrow viewport sizes and at increased text zoom. Status/progress, roll result, errors, and speaker identity are not conveyed by color alone; semantic labels are available to assistive technology. Check contrast, heading order, and announced dynamic updates in each locale.
- The plan does not specify a formal WCAG conformance level or locale-specific defaults; confirm those product choices rather than claiming an unverified level.

## QA boundaries

- **Manual gameplay QA:** Exercise the end-to-end experience in a fictional, explicitly separate QA campaign. Cover campaign creation/switching, multiple sessions and resume, narration/dialogue/activity, a player-click roll, visible panel updates, narrow-screen use, and recovery from a pending or failed turn. Never use, overwrite, or import the vineyard campaign for feature or regression testing.
- **Automated tests:** Use a separate test database, isolated fixtures, a fake GM provider, and a fixed roll source. Assert observable behavior and durable state for campaign isolation, session continuity, turn statuses/idempotency, visibility, D20 flow, locale changes, failure/reconnect recovery, and loading multi-page campaign history without duplicates or losing loaded pages during refresh. Normal automated tests make no live AI calls. Keep the opt-in live-provider smoke check separate from the default suite.
- **Story quality review:** Human-review scenario fixtures for natural consequences, restrained escalation, consistent facts, independent NPC agency, and preservation of player agency. Do not use exact-prose snapshots as the quality bar.
- **Continuity review:** Across multiple sessions, test no unsupported item gain/loss, no accidental duplicate or wrong-owner transfer, no uncommitted location change, and no NPC appearing in an unestablished place. Confirm private facts are present in the GM's prompt and absent from every player-visible projection.
- **Product benchmark:** Compare the player board, inventory model, location/context navigation, secrets, and campaign memory against AI-native tools and system-agnostic campaign managers. Record what is public product documentation, what was hands-on tested, and what is a Storyteller design decision. Use the comparison to improve core tasks, not to chase feature count. The initial public-source and isolated QA review is in [`docs/PRODUCT_BENCHMARK.md`](PRODUCT_BENCHMARK.md); it is a qualitative baseline, not a completed comparative test.
- **Interaction principles:** Apply purpose, player agency, responsibility, familiar mental models, clear feedback, responsive flexibility, simplicity, craft, and delight. Make accepted changes understandable and reversible where safe; keep settings and state-management details close to the content they affect.

## Open design choices to resolve

- Confirm whether sessions are always continuous resumable segments or whether campaign branches are desired; the plan currently selects continuous history.
- Decide campaign-library treatment of archived campaigns and which setup fields are required, optional, or editable after creation.
- Decide how inventory entries are created or edited, which safe item fields are supported, and whether any campaign modes need weight/equipment rules. Do not invent mechanics that a campaign has not chosen.
- Decide how known locations and movement are represented for freeform and map-based campaigns, including when a location is hidden from the player.
- Review whether panel field definitions are player-configurable, which safe field types and visibility choices are offered, and what edit path is supported. Do not add arbitrary executable formulas.
- Confirm any formal accessibility conformance target. The UI-locale default and separation from the campaign narration language are set.
- Confirm the responsive panel arrangement and the visual style of timeline event types and D20 presentation. Do not change the specified roll ownership or invent campaign mechanics to settle these visual choices.
- Evaluate whether the player board keeps current scene, inventory, and resources findable at both wide and narrow sizes. The visual theme is subordinate to a reliable play surface; assistive-technology navigation is V1 work.
- Choose a no-additional-billing source for scene artwork before implementing paced image cards; the existing Plus OAuth response route cannot generate those images.
