# UX acceptance brief

This brief defines observable acceptance for the locally hosted, single-player Storyteller MVP. It follows the committed `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`; it does not define new game mechanics. A screen is accepted when the player can understand what is happening, choose what to do, and recover without losing or duplicating campaign state.

## MVP exit and next product priority

The MVP gate is functional QA: the campaign/session loop, configured local GM connection, actions and questions, time passage, player rolls, tracked state/resources, durable cross-session continuity, bounded context, and recoverable failures must work on separate fictional data. Passing this gate means the core product functions; it does not claim that generated stories are compelling or match the in-chat reference.

After the functional gate, V1's P0 is story quality: engaging scenes with flexible pacing, clear GM ownership of external facts, distinctive characters, smooth handoffs, continuity, and acceptable latency. UI glitches that disrupt play remain active V1 work. Open-source clone/run documentation and a secret/private-data audit follow when the owner considers play polished enough. Version 1.0 ships only after the story-quality bar is met; passing MVP QA alone is not a 1.0 release. See the V1 scenario review and measures in `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`.

## Defining promise: a consistent world, at a deliberate context cost

Storyteller's defining promise is that the campaign stays coherent because the application maintains structured canon, not because the GM model is expected to remember every detail in a long transcript. Persisted characters, places, connections, presence, inventory, and campaign resources are authoritative; narration is accepted only when its proposed changes agree with that canon. The full campaign history remains durable, while each GM request receives only a compact, relevant projection so consistency does not require sending the whole campaign every turn.

- **Travel and presence:** in a fictional vineyard campaign, the Finca and Bodega are distinct places joined by a canonical 40-minute connection. An employee who stays at the Finca cannot speak or act as if physically at the Bodega. Validate structured dialogue and activities against canonical presence, and reject clear public narration claims that place a named off-scene character in the current scene, including a unique distinctive token from a multiword character name. Shared/ambiguous name tokens and common role/title words alone must not trigger rejection. The narration check uses a small English, Spanish, and French cue vocabulary; it is a bounded safeguard, not general language understanding, so ambiguous narration remains a human-review risk. A proposed move must follow a persisted or same-turn accepted route; the application computes and records its duration from the connection instead of trusting a duration asserted by the model. A same-turn newly discovered place, route, and arrival is accepted only when the normalized route exists and validates.
- **Active GM-character duty:** an owner can assign or revise a named active duty for an existing or new GM character only when that character has a known current place. A blank duration means indefinite; a finite duration is authored in in-world minutes and stored as an absolute release minute anchored to the persisted elapsed-world clock at assignment/edit. While the persisted clock is below that threshold, movement to another place fails even with a canonical route. Validate against the pre-turn clock, so a departure's own proposed time advance cannot bypass the duty. Any earlier accepted turn that advances elapsed time to or past the threshold completes it; a subsequent route-valid departure then succeeds and still adds the server-computed route duration. Setting an existing duty's remaining time to 0 completes it at the current stored minute; clearing its name releases it. Same-place actions and unassigned/available characters work normally. Duty details, deadlines, and completion are GM-private and absent from the player board, public projections, and story events. Releasing or changing it requires an out-of-character reason and current campaign revision, creates a private authoring audit entry, and changes neither story history nor game time. Stale revisions and campaigns with an unresolved turn reject duty edits. Backup schema v11 preserves finite-duty thresholds and remote-message paths/events, validates duty locations against the backup clock, and continues to import v1–v10; v9 and earlier treat existing duties as indefinite. Recurring schedules and free-form schedule parsing are not part of this guarantee.
- **Elapsed world time:** the durable campaign clock is an integer minute total, separate from free-form fantasy date/time labels. Every accepted move advances it by at least the server-computed route floor. Sequential legs sum per character; simultaneous characters share elapsed time, so use the largest per-character sum. `time_advance_minutes` is the proposed total turn duration, including travel; commit `max(model total, canonical travel floor)` to avoid counting a narrated route twice. It must be a bounded non-negative integer, positive for Time passage and exactly zero for Ask GM. Failed or rejected turns leave the clock untouched. Show minutes elapsed beneath the current time label; when the accepted date/time label changes, re-anchor the cue to that exact new label. Never parse “First watch” or infer a date rollover.
- **Anchor the opening scene:** a fresh campaign's first GM scene establishes a canonical public place for the player and explicitly places any NPCs who speak or act there. Once a character has a known place, every move to a different place needs a graph route. A legacy character with no known origin can receive a first recorded placement; because there is no prior location to measure from, that initial placement has no computed journey duration.
- **Cross-session consistency:** move a character or item in one session, begin/resume another session, and verify that the board and GM context show the same persisted location, ownership, and quantities. An old transcript line cannot restore stale state or override the canonical record. Verify private places, routes, and details remain excluded from public projections and exports/imports preserve the canon.
- **Sensory authority and player agency:** in a tasting or inspection scene, the GM describes the character's observable evidence first (for a wine tasting, a concise color/aroma/taste profile) and then invites the character's reaction or judgment. The GM must not ask the player to invent what an external thing tastes, looks, sounds, or feels like. The player remains in control of their character's words, actions, and subjective response; those do not silently overwrite the world's properties. When a new sensory finding will matter in later play, preserve it as public continuity; otherwise keep the description in the scene. Human-review this behavior across multiple turns; fake-provider tests cannot establish live narrative quality.
- **Durable story memory:** preserve a concise public fact that is not yet a typed ledger value (for example, the group's agreed work plan). Add or correct it outside the fictional timeline, with an auditable reason. Start a later session and use a fake-provider turn that proposes no continuity changes: the memory must remain on the campaign board and in GM context. It must not create a chat message or advance game time. Unrelated memory must not be injected into every request, and player-visible memory must never include hidden GM-only facts.
- **Indirect social commitments:** save a typed future-meeting and future-reply commitment in one session, then ask about it in a later session with indirect English, Spanish, and French wording. The matching commitment detail reaches the fake provider; same-topic facts and unrelated commitments retain only identity/status metadata. The request remains within the configured 24,000-byte preflight bound. Retrieval uses a small reviewed cue vocabulary, not general semantic search.

## Play screen and player agency

- The play screen presents a readable, chronological timeline that distinguishes player input, GM narration, speaker-attributed NPC dialogue, public remote messages, relevant GM-controlled character activity, and rolls. A remote message has a visibly distinct style and sender label, and its stored event type is not `npc_dialogue`. New completed turns appear without a full-page reload. The initial recent window is bounded; a clear action loads preceding campaign history in chronological order until the earliest event is reached.
- The play surface foregrounds the character's immediate situation and campaign-relevant facts. Interface preferences such as English/Spanish/French and the connected-account GM model belong in Settings, not beside the story as a primary action. The model choice must be drawn from the connected account's available catalog, persist locally, default to Automatic, and apply to new turn resolutions; controls stay off the play board. Account settings identify the effective model and state clearly that reasoning effort is currently left at the model default. In the story, show canonical game date/time at the first event of each turn, then repeat it within that turn only when the clock changes. Keep those markers correct across sessions and when older pages load; never show real-world timestamps. Avoid other repeated labels, setup/admin details, and state already clear from the board; keep a direct path to deeper reference when the player wants it.
- Looking around, tasting, or inspecting something reports what the character can perceive from their current public vantage before asking what the character makes of it. In a tasting, the GM owns the wine's sensory profile and describes it before inviting the player's reaction; never ask the player to author the world's taste or other external facts. Keep the player's character in control of their interpretation, speech, and next action. These answers do not replay the scene, repeat the situation panel, or echo the previous answer. A follow-up observation adds at most one supported new detail; when canon is sparse, the GM may add one brief, non-actionable ambient impression about light, indistinct sound, smell, or general feel when consistent with the known place, time, and weather. Keep ambient details source-free. Never invent a person, item, exit/route, hazard, clue, service, or other actionable fact; those require accepted canon, with accepted presence for a person. If an action depends on an untracked feature, the GM states uncertainty or asks. Ask GM leaves fictional time and canonical state unchanged.
- Small scene cues help ground time and conditions in the imagined world (for example, a moon behind clouds with mist at midnight). Time and weather cues are independent: show the known sun/moon when weather is unknown, and recognized cloud/mist/rain/snow when time is unknown; use a fully neutral cue only when neither is recognized. Story entries arrive gently in the already-paced sequence, and a changed panel value gets a brief cue in place. Keep words authoritative and readable; imagery and motion support the game instead of demanding attention.
- NPC speech is visually attributed to its speaker. Visible activity communicates what relevant NPCs do; it does not reveal private facts, hidden motives, offstage events, or other GM-only state.
- A compact header shows current player-visible world state, including location and in-world date/time and weather where available. Configured campaign panels show their labelled, typed, unit-aware visible fields; the vineyard may use cash, wine inventory, and vine inventory. Panels must not expose private values.
- Resource labels, units, numbers, and long or multiline text stay readable in both the play sidebar and campaign detail. Preserve line breaks inside the value only; do not expose template indentation or add large gaps around receipts and correction links. Keep GM-private values absent.
- Public tracked resources with a saved change can expand a collapsed “Last changed” detail beside the value. It shows the accepted before/after, public reason, and in-world time. Private panel values and private reasons never appear in that detail; no state-change receipt is added to the story timeline.
- In a resource-focused campaign, an accepted sale can update both cash and stock through configured panels. The player sees each current value and its receipt beside the relevant ledger, with no system chat entry. After starting a later session, the board and next GM request use the updated balances. This exercise must not depend on vineyard-specific code.
- Public place and character changes can expand a collapsed “Last changed” detail on their relevant board cards. Show only accepted public state: a place's creation, a character's visible fact update, or a character's move between known public places, with before/after values, an available safe reason, and in-world time. Exclude GM-private places, presence, facts, and reasons; keep these receipts in panels rather than chat.
- Each date, time, and weather fact has one canonical value across the GM's hidden context, the compact header, and the scene board. Legacy aliases and conflicting persisted values must not create duplicate or contradictory facts in play.
- A player can find a concise character board during the scene: current place first, the public places reachable by one canonical route with their in-world travel time, the latest public GM narration as the immediate situation, character details they know, current companions' visible activity, and owned inventory/resources. Names and current public activity of canonically co-present companions appear directly on the scene card without opening place lore; show at most three there and keep any larger cast behind a collapsed continuation. Nearby-place display must filter both private routes and private destination places. The latest narration updates as play continues and never includes GM-private context. This should answer “where am I, what is happening, who is with me, what do I carry or control?” without reading the entire timeline first.
- Keep the player character's name and public description visible in a compact “Your character” card beside the scene and inventory. Show up to three stable public character details at a glance and disclose any additional details only when expanded. Changes cue that card in place; GM-private facts never enter it. A mobile section link makes the card directly reachable without scrolling through the timeline.
- The campaign premise remains available as an expandable reference during play, without leaving the session or repeating it in the event timeline.
- The Plus OAuth game-master route supports streamed text but not generated scene images. Do not add paid API image calls implicitly; scene illustration remains an open integration for a local image generator or player-provided campaign art.
- Campaigns can model distinct carried items and consumables with stable identity, owner, quantity, unit, and any campaign-relevant safe details. Resource ledgers also support non-item values such as vineyard cash, wine stock, and vines. The same interaction works for different campaign genres without hardcoded vineyard or dungeon rules.
- A text composer lets the player submit an action or speech. Pending submission prevents accidental duplicates and makes the submitted text and resulting turn status understandable.
- Contextual play nudges include looking around, asking a direct question, waiting at the current place, and explicitly letting time pass. Let time pass includes a **Pass a few days** nudge that stops at the next meaningful player decision; the player can still request a custom duration in the composer. Time passage preserves a duration the player stated, including multiple days, and may advance world or NPC activity; it never chooses or narrates the player's actions, speech, thoughts, movement, or rolls. The structured clock contract in the defining-promise acceptance checks governs elapsed minutes; the calendar remains the campaign's free-form label. Once the first streamed text arrives, the wait status gently advances to show the GM has begun composing. New GM, NPC, and world beats reveal in a paced sequence with a clear show-all control. Initial history is not replayed, and loading older history preserves the reader's position.

A completed response feels like one coherent GM turn, not four or five abrupt, disjointed messages. When a character has related things to say, combine them into one concise utterance and speech bubble; avoid filler and repeated lines. Do not add system-style chat entries for memory, inventory, location, resources, objective status, or character-record mutations. Clearly ongoing work or an explicit interval resolves in one flowing scene summary at the requested scale, including meaningful progress and relevant reactions by characters who are present; routine micro-actions are not serialized into a prompt after every step. Sensory facts are established by the GM before asking for the player's interpretation. If the player's scope is unclear, the GM stops after the immediate consequence. Fake-provider tests cover prompt guidance and state boundaries, not live prose quality.

Keep the essential world rail visible; clamp its immediate-situation preview while the full narration remains in story history. Durable private GM context remains hidden from every player-facing projection, and public resolutions/retractions remain traceable without exposing private reasoning.

The UI never writes the player's actions, speech, thoughts, or choices on their behalf. It does not present a proposed state change as canonical before the application accepts it.
- Location, character presence, inventory ownership, and resource quantities are canonical persisted state. Narration alone cannot change them: accepted state deltas reference stable records, are validated, committed once with their turn, and appear in a traceable history. If a turn records multiple player movements, the final canonical place and world location agree in the player board and later GM context. Each resource history entry shows the field, before and after values, operation, unit, and grounded reason. A read-only account review creates no balance change.

## Player-click D20 flow

- If an uncertain, consequential player action needs a roll, the timeline states what is being tested and the difficulty or target before asking for the player's roll. Ordinary actions do not trigger a roll merely to add activity.
- The player initiates the roll with an explicit D20 click. The interface clearly indicates when a roll is awaited, accepted, and resolved; the recorded result is visible in the turn history. The server-generated result is applied exactly once.
- The GM does not silently roll for a player-controlled character. The current MVP lets the player initiate their own D20 result; add a distinct, auditable GM roll only if a campaign's rules require it.

## Progress, failure, and reconnect

- While a GM response is in progress, show a clear pending/progress state and keep the player from submitting a duplicate action. Do not imply that a turn is complete before the response is validated and committed.
- A slow but actively streaming GM response remains the same saved turn across reconnects and never starts a parallel retry. Give response-stream reads a substantially longer idle grace than OAuth/catalog requests, and renew the resolution lease from stream activity with attempt fencing. If the stream goes idle past its receive timeout, fail that same turn recoverably; do not append partial text or apply canon.
- If a submitted action arrives just after another turn became active, or a ChatGPT plan pause raced with submission, keep the unsaved text in the composer and explain why it was not accepted. Do not create a second turn or send the preserved draft automatically when the earlier turn completes or requests resume; the player chooses when to send it.
- On timeout, invalid response, or other recoverable failure, explain that resolution did not complete, preserve the prior canonical state, and offer a clear retry path. For a malformed or rejected GM reply, make clear that no part of that reply was added to the story or state; never expose raw model output or private prompt context. State that retry continues the same saved action; if the player already rolled, say the saved result will be reused. Retrying does not duplicate the action, event, roll, or state change.
- When required context remains above Storyteller's local serialized-byte limit after safe event-prose compaction, preserve canon and the saved action, make no provider request, and explain that this is a local request-size limit rather than an account usage pause. Offer a practical way to reduce unusually long authored instructions/notes and retry the same turn; keep this notice translated in all supported locales.
- If a turn is waiting for a player roll, a refresh or reconnect restores that same pending turn and roll request. If a turn completed, reconnect restores the completed timeline and current state. The player should not have to infer whether an action was lost or applied twice.
- When account usage prevents a GM request, show one clear account-wide pause with a link to usage settings and an explicit Resume requests action. This is the only visible explanation of the account limit. Keep the failed turn calm and focused on the preserved action or opening scene; show that same action in the story and keep its Retry this turn control in place but disabled until the player resumes requests. Remove secondary pause warnings from the composer and avoid repeating the limit in status announcements. Resuming only clears the pause; it must not call the provider. After resume, enable retry for the same saved turn, preserve any D20 result and canonical state, and wait for the player to retry explicitly. Do not imply a specific reset time, fictionalize the limit as game energy, or silently switch to paid API usage. Other campaigns may still attempt requests against the shared plan allowance.
